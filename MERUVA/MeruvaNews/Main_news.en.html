Why we roast slowly, batch by batch

Fast roasting is convenient, but it loses the layers of aroma. The story of 14 minutes per batch and why MERUVA doesn't rush.

There is a faster way to roast coffee — push the heat very high, cut the time, pull a batch in 8 minutes. Convenient for volume, but the beans are stopped before they fully "bloom" into their layers of aroma.

At MERUVA, each batch runs about 14 minutes. We follow the roast curve closely so the beans develop evenly from the inside out: the drying phase, the yellowing, then first crack — where the sweetness and fruit notes of Đà Lạt coffee truly reveal themselves.

Slow, to keep what?

  • Complex layers of aroma — a clean finish, never harsh.
  • Natural sweetness instead of a scorched taste covering it up.
  • Consistency from batch to batch.

Roasting slowly isn't for show — it's the most honest way to treat the bean. From the summit, we serve with patience.

"When it's gone, it's gone" — the philosophy of a limited batch

Why doesn't MERUVA over-bake just to keep selling? The story of the 7 a.m. croissant and freshness without compromise.

Many bakeries over-bake to "keep selling" until the evening. MERUVA chose the opposite: a limited quantity each day — when it's gone, it's gone.

Croissants come out of the oven at 7 a.m., the crust still audibly crisp when you break it. The dough is cold-proofed overnight, real butter, no freshness additives. A pastry left until the afternoon is no longer the pastry we want you to eat.

A limit is a promise

When we say "when it's gone, it's gone", we promise that whatever you hold is always at its very best. That is kindness to the person eating — and to the batch itself.
